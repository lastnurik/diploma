**Status, 22 October 2026:** This is a preliminary rationale for the [project proposal](../proposal.md). No survey responses, user interviews, client agreement or product trials have been documented. The [draft questionnaire](questionnaire.md) has not been administered. Conclusions about user demand and the final requirements depend on direct input and feasibility analysis, provisionally scheduled through late November to mid-December.

## Problem and proposed contribution

The project addresses the gap between formulating a game concept and producing an executable game for creators who do not have development expertise. Its proposed AI-assisted workflow converts a user-authored narrative and selected game preferences into a **playable 2D browser game**, without requiring the user to program or operate a game engine. This entails translating incomplete narrative descriptions into game state, coherent player actions, progression rules and a reachable ending. Whether the intended users value this workflow, and which compromises in fidelity or complexity they would accept, requires empirical investigation. Writers, educators and small creative teams are candidate groups rather than validated user segments.

Existing products illustrate relevant but distinct authoring workflows. [Rosebud AI's tutorial](https://rosebud.ai/tutorial) describes prompt creation, playable preview, iteration and publishing; [GDevelop's AI Agent](https://gdevelop.io/blog/make-games-with-ai-agent-gdevelop-automated-prompt) edits objects and event logic within an existing project; [AI Dungeon's guide](https://help.aidungeon.com/getting-started) describes open-ended interactive storytelling. These are official product descriptions, not independent performance comparisons. The proposed study will examine the feasibility and usability of generating a complete browser-playable game from a user-provided narrative, with explicit handling of unsupported mechanics and separate assessment of playability, completion and narrative correspondence. A detailed comparison of these systems is planned for the next phase; no unique advantage is established here.

Wang et al.'s [ByteSized32 study](https://aclanthology.org/2023.emnlp-main.830/) distinguishes the execution, fidelity and winnability of generated text games. Although its setting differs from 2D browser games based on user-authored narratives, these dimensions inform separate evaluation criteria. Published [human–AI interaction guidelines](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/guidelines-for-human-ai-interaction/) support communicating system capabilities and failures clearly. Neither source measures demand for this platform or predicts its technical performance. The broader literature review remains future research.

## Hypotheses and next evidence

| Question to test | Focused input needed before fixing requirements |
| --- | --- |
| Which prospective creators would use a generated browser game, and for what purpose? | Ask candidate users about their concepts, technical experience, prior creation attempts, barriers and expected outputs; record roles, dates, anonymized statements and divergent views. Distinguish stated interest from observed use. |
| Which story elements and mechanics must survive simplification? | Ask users to rank premise, central actions and ending against examples that replace unsupported mechanics; compare judgments rather than assume one genre-independent rule. |
| What constitutes an acceptable generated game? | Discuss a small, manually authored playable example and several story scenarios; assess the clarity of controls, objectives, outcomes and failure explanations. Evaluate actual generated outputs separately at a later stage. |
| What feasible technical boundary should the team promise? | Complete a critical alternatives and literature review, agree a bounded mechanic set and verification criteria with the supervisor, then revise the proposal and requirements in light of the evidence. |

The October checkpoint consists of the proposal, this preliminary relevance analysis and a minimal repository with a task record. During November and early December, the team plans to collect and analyze user or client input, examine technical feasibility, and document how the findings affect scope. Requirements are provisionally scheduled for agreement in late November to mid-December. The proposed treatment of unsupported mechanics and separate playability and completion checks remain design hypotheses pending comparative and prototype evaluation.
