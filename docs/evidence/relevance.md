## Why the project matters

A creator may have a story but lack the programming and game-engine skills to turn it into a game. The proposed platform would generate a playable 2D browser game with coherent actions and a reachable ending. Writers, educators and small creative teams are candidate users, **not validated segments**. Direct research must establish their needs and acceptable simplifications.

Related tools already cover parts of this workflow: [Rosebud AI](https://rosebud.ai/tutorial) generates browser-game previews from prompts; [GDevelop](https://gdevelop.io/blog/make-games-with-ai-agent-gdevelop-automated-prompt) helps edit explicit game rules; [AI Dungeon](https://help.aidungeon.com/getting-started) supports open-ended text adventures. The [three-system comparison](../existing-systems.md) is based on product documentation, not independent trials. The proposed research focus is a bounded story-to-game contract with visible simplifications and separate checks for launch, actions, ending and story fidelity. Its advantage remains to be tested.

[ByteSized32](https://aclanthology.org/2023.emnlp-main.830/) distinguishes whether generated text games run, match intent and can be won; [human–AI guidelines](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/guidelines-for-human-ai-interaction/) motivate clear limitations and failures. Neither study measures demand for this platform. See the [literature review](../literature.md).

## Supplied questionnaire export

The 142-row export reports 98 records (69.0%) with no or beginner programming/game-engine experience. An immediately playable browser game was the most selected single outcome at 65 (45.8%), a plurality rather than a majority. For trying a future prototype, 57 (40.1%) selected yes, 48 (33.8%) maybe and 37 (26.1%) no. Translating a story into gameplay rules (48; 33.8%) and programming (47; 33.1%) were the most selected obstacles. These are descriptive distributions in a supplied file. The form link and questionnaire are recorded in [the instrument note](questionnaire.md); recruitment, consent records, distinct participation and respondent identity have not been independently verified. No raw responses or free-text answers are published in this repository. Do not describe the records as confirmed student participants without an auditable collection account; do not infer actual adoption from stated interest.

## What must be learned next

The questionnaire suggests topics to investigate, not settled requirements: which creators would use the tool, which story actions must survive simplification, and what counts as a complete game. In the supplied records, main player action (49; 34.5%) and achievable ending (45; 31.7%) were the most selected quality priorities; loss of the central idea (46; 32.4%) and crashes (45; 31.7%) were the most selected unacceptable outcomes. These proportions inherit the export's provenance limits.

The team plans to audit that provenance, collect the required direct user/client input, test a bounded mechanic set, and revise requirements with actual findings and supervisor feedback in late November to mid-December. No prototype usability or generated-game performance result exists yet. The [supervisor record](../supervisor-feedback.md) describes the scope guidance.
