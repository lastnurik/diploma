## Problem and proposed contribution

The project addresses the gap between formulating a game concept and producing an executable game for creators who do not have development expertise. Its proposed AI-assisted workflow converts a user-authored narrative and selected game preferences into a **playable 2D browser game**, without requiring the user to program or operate a game engine. This entails translating incomplete narrative descriptions into game state, coherent player actions, progression rules and a reachable ending. Whether the intended users value this workflow, and which compromises in fidelity or complexity they would accept, requires empirical investigation. Writers, educators and small creative teams are candidate groups rather than validated user segments.

Existing products illustrate relevant but distinct authoring workflows. [Rosebud AI's tutorial](https://rosebud.ai/tutorial) describes prompt creation, playable preview, iteration and publishing; [GDevelop's AI Agent](https://gdevelop.io/blog/make-games-with-ai-agent-gdevelop-automated-prompt) edits objects and event logic within an existing project; [AI Dungeon's guide](https://help.aidungeon.com/getting-started) describes open-ended interactive storytelling. These are official product descriptions, not independent performance comparisons. The proposed study will examine the feasibility and usability of generating a complete browser-playable game from a user-provided narrative, with explicit handling of unsupported mechanics and separate assessment of playability, completion and narrative correspondence. The repository contains a [desk comparison of three existing systems](../existing-systems.md) using shared criteria. It is based on published descriptions rather than hands-on trials; no unique advantage or comparative performance benefit is established.

Wang et al.'s [ByteSized32 study](https://aclanthology.org/2023.emnlp-main.830/) distinguishes the execution, fidelity and winnability of generated text games. Although its setting differs from 2D browser games based on user-authored narratives, these dimensions inform separate evaluation criteria. Published [human–AI interaction guidelines](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/guidelines-for-human-ai-interaction/) support communicating system capabilities and failures clearly. Neither source measures demand for this platform or predicts its technical performance. A [critical literature review](../literature.md) provides a research basis, not evidence of user demand or evaluated prototype performance.

## Supplied questionnaire export

The 142-row export reports 98 records (69.0%) with no or beginner programming/game-engine experience. An immediately playable browser game was the most selected single outcome at 65 (45.8%), a plurality rather than a majority. For trying a future prototype, 57 (40.1%) selected yes, 48 (33.8%) maybe and 37 (26.1%) no. Translating a story into gameplay rules (48; 33.8%) and programming (47; 33.1%) were the most selected obstacles. These are descriptive distributions in a supplied file. The form link and questionnaire are recorded in [the instrument note](questionnaire.md); recruitment, consent records, distinct participation and respondent identity have not been independently verified. No raw responses or free-text answers are published in this repository. Do not describe the records as confirmed student participants without an auditable collection account; do not infer actual adoption from stated interest.

## Hypotheses and next evidence

| Question to test | Focused input needed before fixing requirements |
| --- | --- |
| Which prospective creators would use a generated browser game, and for what purpose? | Audit the export's provenance and creator-role subset, then seek direct input from intended users. Distinguish stated interest from observed use. |
| Which story elements and mechanics must survive simplification? | The supplied records most often select main player action (49; 34.5%) and an achievable ending (45; 31.7%) as quality priorities. Test this with intended users and actual story examples. |
| What constitutes an acceptable generated game? | The supplied records most often flag a lost central idea (46; 32.4%) and crashes (45; 31.7%) as unacceptable. Assess actual playability and narrative correspondence separately after a prototype exists. |
| What feasible technical boundary should the team promise? | Review the drafted literature and technology comparisons, test feasibility assumptions, and agree a bounded mechanic set and verification criteria with the supervisor; revise the proposed requirements in light of the findings. |
